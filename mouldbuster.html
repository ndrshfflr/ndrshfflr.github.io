Sporexplore: mouldbuster

Microscopy-assisted identification for indoor Penicillium & Aspergillus — the moulds most often implicated in water-damaged buildings.
Not a diagnostic tool. Reliable mould identification requires culturing on standard media (MEA/CYA) at controlled temperature and a calibrated microscope. This app narrows candidates from morphology you can observe — it does not replace a mycology lab, and it should never be used to rule out health-relevant species. If anyone in the building has respiratory symptoms, or the affected area is large, get a professional assessment.

Colony and microscopy figures compiled from standard mycological references (Pitt & Hocking, Fungi and Food Spoilage; Samson et al., Food and Indoor Fungi) and cross-checked against Mycology Online (Adelaide) and DoctorFungus. Eight species, first release — flag any species you need added.

Photos are hotlinked from openly-licensed collections — CDC Public Health Image Library (public domain) and iNaturalist / GBIF / Mushroom Observer / Landcare Research NZ (CC BY or CC BY-NC, credited per image). We could not find freely-licensed photographs specifically on MEA, CYA, or V8 agar for most species — that level of specificity mostly lives in paywalled taxonomic monographs. Where the medium is known it's stated in the caption (mostly SDA for the CDC Aspergillus cultures); several Penicillium photos are field shots on natural substrate rather than lab culture.
