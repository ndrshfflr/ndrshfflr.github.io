Slowly, we drove our campervan through the narrow serpentine roads cutting through the landscape. Smålandets Markaryds Älgsafari is one of many for-profit animal parks in Sweden where you can see these giants in the flesh for a modest fee.

It was a rash decision prompted by a large advertising board that drew me to the park. My partner and I briefly discussed the ethics behind it as we generally do not support keeping large animals in captivity. Our first stop led us to a sketchy place that seemed rather unwelcoming, so we continued 50 km south and found Smålandets Markaryds Älgsafari. In comparison to the first place, it seemed much better and gave us peace of heart.

At first glance the elk seemed unbothered by our presence − even when we got as close as just a few metres. But when the excitement wore off, it dawned on me that they were giving us a constant watchfull eye. Uknown to me exactly how many, I estimate around 20 to 30 elk reside in the 15 hectare (0.15 km2) enclosure. In the wild, their ranges vary from region to region, but a 2013 study showed elk browse home ranges spanning from 41.6 to 458.9 km2. Comparing these numbers, the captured elk are given an area that is – at best – roughly 0.3% the size of their natural range. At worst, they have to make due with an area as small as 0.03% of their natural tendency. For a Stockholm citizen, that would mean you are allowed to browse an maximal area of 0.56 km2. That means you would never be allowed to roam more than 50-500m away from your dwelling. Your entire life.
